1.0 Project Overview • Introduction
Centralizing athletic operations, memberships, facilities, and competitive tournaments
FOUNDATION
📌 Background & Motivation
Modern athletic facilities and sports clubs operate across diverse disciplines (Cricket, Football, Tennis, Swimming, Badminton, Basketball). Managing these operations requires coordinating hundreds of athlete memberships, certified coaches, training facility slots, subscription fee collections, and tournament schedules.
  • Multi-Disciplinary Scope: Unified platform managing athletes across 6+ sports simultaneously.
  • Relational Paradigm: Eliminates legacy spreadsheet islands and paper logs with ACID-compliant storage.
  • Role-Based Access: Dedicated privileges for System Administrators, Coaches, and Athlete Members.
  • Data Integrity & Automation: Guaranteed atomicity in financial transactions and automated schedule overlap prevention.
    • ⚡ Core Operational Domains
  • Athlete Profiles & Authentication: Demographic data, SHA-256 password hashing, and membership lifecycle states (Active, Expired, Suspended).
  • Club Franchises & Tactical Rosters: Many-to-Many team enrollments with specific positional roles (Striker, Wicketkeeper, Point Guard).
  • Multi-Tier Subscriptions & Billing: Flexible plan tiers (Monthly, Quarterly, Gold Pass) with multi-mode payment auditing.
  • Training & Facility Scheduling: Ground/court reservations with capacity tracking and conflict avoidance.
  • Tournament Brackets & Match Fixtures: League/knockout match organization with automated standings computation.
    • 2.0 Problem Statement • Operational Challenges
    Overcoming critical anomalies and bottlenecks in traditional club administration
    CHALLENGES
    ❌ Data Redundancy & Anomalies
    Insertion & Deletion Anomalies:

    In un-normalized schemas, coach contact details or sport definitions cannot be added without enrolling a member, leading to NULL values and inconsistent records.


    Update Inconsistencies:

    Modifying a coach's phone number across multiple spreadsheet sheets often leaves orphaned or contradictory records.

    ⚠️ Scheduling & Venue Overlaps
    Double-Booking Facilities:

    Simultaneous training sessions and tournament matches scheduled at the same stadium turf or indoor arena create major operational collisions.


    Sport Incompatibility:

    Without database validation triggers, tournament fixtures can mistakenly pair teams registered under different sports disciplines.

    💳 Financial & Roster Blindspots
    Uncollected Subscription Fees:

    Lack of atomic transaction linking allows athletes with expired dues to continue booking premium training sessions.


    Untracked Player Roster Roles:

    No historical tracking of tactical positions or membership duration across multiple clubs.

    3.0 Project Objectives • System Scope
    Architectural goals and engineering scope for the relational database system
    OBJECTIVES
    🎯 Primary Project Objectives
  • 3NF Normalization: Decompose data into 12 atomic relations with zero transitive dependencies to guarantee mathematical data integrity.
  • Enforce Referential & Domain Constraints: Utilize Foreign Keys, composite primary keys, and CHECK constraints for all vital fields.
  • Automated Business Integrity Triggers: Implement procedural triggers preventing venue booking collisions and tournament team sport mismatches.
  • Sub-Millisecond Query Performance: Pre-compile analytical views (`v_club_rosters`, `v_tournament_standings`, `v_facility_utilization`) for high throughput.
  • Full-Stack Interoperability: Seamless interface with Python HTTP backend and interactive Single-Page Application (SPA).

    RELATIONAL SCHEMA
    📝 Formal Relational Notation (3NF)
    MEMBER (member_id, name, gender, phone, email, password, address, join_date, status) SPORT (sport_id, sport_name) MEMBERSHIP_PLAN (plan_id, plan_name, duration_months, fee_amount) FACILITY (facility_id, name, type, location, capacity, status) COACH (coach_id, name, phone, email) CLUB (club_id, club_name, created_date, status, sport_id, coach_id) CLUB_MEMBER (member_id, club_id, role, join_date) PAYMENT (payment_id, member_id, plan_id, amount, payment_mode, payment_date, status, transaction_ref) TRAINING_SESSION (session_id, session_date, start_time, end_time, capacity, status, sport_id, coach_id, venue_id) TEAM (team_id, team_name, created_date, status, club_id, sport_id, coach_id) TOURNAMENT (tournament_id, name, start_date, end_date, type, sport_id) FIXTURE (fixture_id, fixture_date, status, tournament_id, team1_id, team2_id, venue_id)
    🔗 Cardinality & Relationship Rules
  • MEMBER ↔ CLUB (N:M): An athlete can enroll in multiple clubs with distinct tactical roles; a club holds multiple athletes. Resolved by CLUB_MEMBER.
  • MEMBER ↔ PAYMENT (1:N): An athlete performs multiple subscription transactions over time; each payment record belongs to exactly one athlete.
  • SPORT ↔ CLUB (1:N): Each franchise is dedicated to one sport discipline; a sport hosts multiple competing clubs.
  • FACILITY ↔ TRAINING_SESSION (1:N): A venue hosts multiple time slots sequentially; each session occupies one venue.
  • TOURNAMENT ↔ FIXTURE (1:N): A tournament organizes a bracket of matches between competing squads (team1_id ≠ team2_id).

    8.0 Database Integrity • Triggers & Constraints
    Active procedural integrity enforcement and ACID compliance rules
    INTEGRITY
    🛡️ Declarative Integrity Rules
  • Entity Integrity: Primary Keys enforce uniqueness on all entities (`member_id`, `club_id`, `payment_id`, `session_id`).
  • Referential Integrity: Foreign Keys guarantee orphan-free child records with `ON DELETE RESTRICT / CASCADE`.
  • Domain Constraints: Check conditions restrict values (e.g., `amount > 0`, `end_time > start_time`, valid payment modes).
  • Unique Constraints: Prevents duplicate registration of emails (`member.email`, `coach.email`) and transaction reference numbers.
    • ⚡ Procedural Trigger: Sport Validation
    -- Ensures competing squads participate in tournament sport CREATE TRIGGER trg_check_fixture_team_sport BEFORE INSERT ON fixture FOR EACH ROW BEGIN SELECT CASE WHEN (SELECT sport_id FROM team WHERE team_id = NEW.team1_id) <> (SELECT sport_id FROM tournament WHERE tournament_id = NEW.tournament_id) THEN RAISE(ABORT, 'Error: Team 1 sport mismatch!') WHEN (SELECT sport_id FROM team WHERE team_id = NEW.team2_id) <> (SELECT sport_id FROM tournament WHERE tournament_id = NEW.tournament_id) THEN RAISE(ABORT, 'Error: Team 2 sport mismatch!') END; END;
